Your First Anime Convention in India: What to Plan Before You Go

In brief

Prepare for an anime convention or fan event with a realistic budget, travel plan, consent awareness and clear expectations.

Key takeaways

  • Verify current event details with the organiser.
  • Budget for travel and purchases before you go.
  • Respect consent and other attendees.

Verify the event

Start with the organiser's official page and confirm the event name, venue, date, schedule, ticket terms and contact details. Social posts can be outdated or unofficial. Do not assume a guest appearance or venue is confirmed unless the organiser states it.

Check ticket inclusions, refund rules, entry timings and whether registration is required for particular activities. Save the confirmation and event contact details where you can access them on the day.

Set a full budget

Include transport, food, water, accommodation if needed, merchandise and a small emergency buffer. Decide in advance how much you can spend so a limited-edition stall or social pressure does not derail the budget.

Check public transport timing and the route back. Share your plan with someone you trust and avoid relying on an unverified last-minute ride or accommodation offer.

Plan what you want to do

Review the schedule and mark a few priority activities instead of trying to attend everything. Allow time for queues, breaks and unexpected delays. If attending with friends, agree on a meeting point in case mobile data is unreliable.

For cosplay, check the event's costume, accessory and photography rules. A policy accepted at one event may not apply at another, so read the current organiser guidance.

Respect consent and accessibility

Ask before photographing or filming cosplayers and other attendees. A public event does not mean every person has agreed to be featured on your social account. Respect a no and do not touch costumes without permission.

Check venue accessibility, seating, washrooms and crowd conditions that matter to you. If you need an accommodation, contact the organiser before the event rather than assuming it will be available.

Stay safe and handle problems

Keep ticket details and emergency contacts accessible. Follow venue staff instructions and use official help points if you lose something or feel unsafe. Avoid sharing personal information with strangers you have just met.

If something differs from the listing, document the issue and contact the organiser through the published channel. Avoid spreading an unverified allegation before checking the facts.

After the event

Keep receipts and note which activities were worth the time and money. Share photos only with consent and credit artists when posting their work. If you give feedback, be specific about queues, access, communication or scheduling.

The best first convention is not the one where you buy the most. It is one where you know what to expect, have a manageable plan and enjoy the community without exceeding your budget.

Verify the event details before making travel plans

Convention dates, venues, guest lists, ticket tiers, and entry rules can change. Use the organiser’s official website or verified social account to confirm the date, venue, ticket seller, refund terms, and event schedule. A reposted poster or an old search result may refer to a previous edition. If travel or accommodation is involved, check the organiser’s latest announcements before booking non-refundable arrangements.

Read what each ticket includes. A general-entry pass may not cover workshops, meet-and-greets, special screenings, or priority queues. Check entry times, age requirements, identity requirements if any, and whether re-entry is permitted. Keep the confirmation and payment receipt accessible offline in case mobile data is unreliable at the venue.

Plan your day around priorities

List the one or two things you most want to do: meet a creator, attend a panel, shop for art, participate in cosplay, or spend time with friends. Mark their scheduled times and allow for queueing, walking, food, and rest. Do not plan every minute around optimistic travel estimates. Large venues and crowded halls can make it difficult to reach a session exactly at its start.

If you are travelling from another city, account for transport to the venue and the journey home. Check the last available train, bus, or metro service relevant to your route, and make a backup plan if the event ends late. Share your basic itinerary with someone you trust, especially if you are attending alone.

Set a shopping budget and check seller details

Convention stalls can make impulse purchases tempting. Decide on a maximum spend before entering and separate it from travel and food money. Compare an item’s price, condition, size, and seller information before buying. Ask whether a product is officially licensed, handmade, or fan-made, and look for clear descriptions rather than assuming that every item featuring a character is official merchandise. Keep receipts for higher-value purchases and review the event’s rules about returns or disputes.

Cosplay and comfort need practical planning

Review the current event policy for props, weapons replicas, large accessories, and costume changes. Rules differ by organiser and venue. Make sure your costume allows you to see, breathe, walk, sit, and use stairs safely. Carry water where permitted, comfortable footwear or a change of shoes if practical, and a small repair kit. If you need to change clothes, check whether the venue provides a designated area rather than assuming a private space will be available.

Ask before photographing cosplayers, performers, or attendees. A public event is not permission to touch someone’s costume, post close-up photographs, or record private conversations. Respect “no photos” requests and the venue’s photography rules.

Prepare for crowds and accessibility needs

Check venue information for entry queues, accessible routes, seating, quiet areas, washrooms, food, and permitted bags. If you have accessibility needs, contact the organiser early to ask about the actual arrangements rather than relying on assumptions. Keep essential medication and emergency contacts accessible. Agree on a meeting point with friends in case phone signals fail or the group becomes separated.

First-convention checklist

  • Confirm the date, venue, ticket seller, schedule, and entry conditions.
  • Save your ticket and organiser updates for offline access.
  • Choose priority activities and leave time for queues and breaks.
  • Set a shopping budget separate from travel and essentials.
  • Review cosplay, prop, and photography rules.
  • Plan transport home and a backup contact.
  • Respect consent and venue staff instructions.

A good first convention is not measured by how much you buy or how many sessions you attend. It is one where you feel prepared, spend within your limits, respect other attendees, and leave with a few meaningful experiences.
